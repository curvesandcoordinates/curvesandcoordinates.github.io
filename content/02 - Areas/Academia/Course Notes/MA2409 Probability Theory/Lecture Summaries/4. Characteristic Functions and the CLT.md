# Characteristic Functions

> [!definition] Characteristic Functions \[p.26]
> Given a Random Variable $X$ and $t \in \R$ we define the chracteristic function of $X$ as the expectation $\phi_{X}(t) = \mathbb{E}[e^{itX}]$

> [!prompt]- Why must the expectation of the characteristic function always exist?
> Because $e^{itX}$ is a bounded random variable.

> [!lemma] Basic Properties of Characteristic Functions \[p. 26]
>
> 1. $\phi_{X}$ is determined only by the distribution of $X$
> 2. $\phi_{X}(0) = 1$
> 3. $|\phi_{X}(t)| \leq 1$ for any $t \in \R$
> 4. $\phi_{X}$ is continuous in $\R$
> 5. If $\mathbb{E}(X) < \infty$ then $\phi_{X} \in C^{1}$ and $\phi_{X}'(0) = i \mathbb{E}(X)$ and $|\phi_{X}'(t)| \leq E(|X|)$ for any $t$
> 6. If ${} \mathbb{E}(X^2) < \infty {}$ then $\phi_{X} \in C^{2}$ and $\phi_{X}''(0) = -\mathbb{E}(X^{2})$ and ${} |\phi_{X}''(t)| \leq E(X^{2}) {}$ for any $t$
> 7. For independent $X$ and $Y$ we get $\phi_{X+Y}(t) = \phi_{X}(t) \cdot \phi_{Y}(t)$ for every $t$
> 8. For $M \in \R$ we have $\phi_{MX}(t) = \phi_{X}(Mt)$
> 9. If $X$ and $-X$ have the same distribution (symmetry) then $\phi_{X}(t) \in \R$

## Main Use of the Characteristic Functions

> [!Lemma] Convergence in Distribution iff Pointwise Convergence
> Given a sequence of Random variables $\set{X_{n}}_{n \in \N}$ and a variable $X$ we get that
> $X_{n} \overset{Dist}{\to} X \implies \forall t \in \R: \quad \lim_{t \to \infty} \phi_{X_{n}}(t) = \phi_{X}(t)$
> and assuming that $\phi_{X}(0) = 1$ and $\phi_{X}$ is continuous at $0$ that
> $\phi_{X_{n}}(t) \to \phi_{X} \implies X_{n} \overset{Dist}{\to} X$

`\begin{proof}`
The first is basically the statement of **Lemma 4.6**

The second is based on the more complicated definition of a tight sequence, but is mainly summarized in **Lemma 4.11**
`\end{proof}`

### A very technical definiton of Tightness

> [!definition] Tightness
> A sequence $X_{n}$ is tight if for every $\epsilon$ there exists $M$ such that
> $\forall n: P(|X_{n}| > M) < \epsilon$

The main Lemmas we need this for is these two

> [!lemma] Tighness of a Sequence of Random Variables
> Let $X_n$ be a tight sequence of random variables. Then there exists a subsequence $X_{n_{k}}$ which converges in distribution

> [!lemma]
> For a given sequence $X_{n}$ with the property that $\phi_{X_{n}}$ that converges pointwise to some $\phi$ with $\phi(0) = 1$ and is continuous at $0$. Then the sequence $X_{n}$ is tight.

# Central Limit Theorem

> [!theorem] Central Limit Theorem
> Let $X_{n}$ be i.i.d. random variables and assume $\mu = \Exp{X_{1}}$ and $\sigma^{2} = \var(X_{1})< \infty$. Then for $S_{n}= \sum\limits_{k=1}^{n} X_{k}$
> $\frac{S_{n} - n \mu}{\sigma \sqrt{n}} \overset{dist}{\to} \mathcal{N}(0,1)$
