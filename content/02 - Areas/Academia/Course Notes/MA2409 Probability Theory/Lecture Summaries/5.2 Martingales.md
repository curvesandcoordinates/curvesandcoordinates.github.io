# Prerequisites

## Filtrations

We want to consider sequences of $\sigma$-algebras that describe a se of information per given step $n$ in a specific way. This is caputres in the idea of a filtration on the $\sigma$-Algebra $\mathcal{F}$.

> [!definition] Filtration
> Given a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ a filtration is a sequence $\set{\mathcal{G}_{n}}$ of $\sigma$-algebras on $\Omega$ such that
>
> 1. For all $n$ we have that $\mathcal{G}_{n} \subset \mathcal{G}_{n+1}$
> 2. For all $n$ : $\mathcal{G}_{n}  \subset \mathcal{F}$

The prime example of a filtration given a sequence of random variables $X_{n}$ is that of
$\mathcal{G}_{n} = \sigma(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n})$
Here the direction of the inclusion is chosen arbitrarily which will lead us to defining inverse filtrations aswell, but one has to note that the interpretation here is simply that the more steps we take in the sequence the more information we have since
$\set{\emptyset, \Omega} \subset \mathcal{F} \subset \mathcal{G}_{1} \subset \cdots \subset\mathcal{G_{n}}$

> [!definition] Inverse Filtration
> Given a probability space $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, P)$ a filtration is a sequence $\set{\mathcal{G}_{n}}$ of $\sigma$-algebras on $\Omega$ such that
>
> 1. For all $n$ we have that $\mathcal{G}_{n+1} \subset \mathcal{G}_{n}$
> 2. For all $n$: $\mathcal{G}_{n}  \subset \mathcal{F}$

## Martingales

We now want to define a sequence of special random variables where the “next step” is given by considering some sequence of information and approximating the previous step.

> [!definition] Martingales
> Let $\set{X_{n}}$ be a sequence of random variables and $\set{\mathcal{G}_{n}}$ a filtration. We call $\set{X_{n}}$ a martingale with respect to $\set{\mathcal{G}_{n}}$ if
>
> 1. $\Exp{|X_{n}|} < \infty$
> 2. $X_{n}$ is measurable with respect to $\mathcal{G_{n}}$ for every $n$
> 3. For any $n$ we have $\Exp{X_{n+1} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n}} = X_{n}$

Keep in mind here that the intuition should be that for any $A \in \mathcal{G}_n$ we have that
$P(\Exp{X_{n+1} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n}} \in A) = P(X_{n} \in A)$
meaning in a sense using the data in $\mathcal{G_{n}}$ we want the next step to not deviate from the last in this information.

There is a whole family of martingale definitions that all only vary in the last property (the martingale property). We will list them now

- **Inverse Martingales:** $\Exp{X_{n} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n+1}} = X_{n+1}$
- **Sub-martingale:** $\Exp{X_{n+1} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n}} \geq X_{n}$
- **Super-martingale:** $\Exp{X_{n+1} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n}} \leq X_{n}$

An important lemma that gives us all the working proeprties of martingales is

> [!lemma] Useful Tools
>
> 1. For a given sequence $X_{n}$ it is a martingale if and only if it is a martingale with respect to its natural filtration $\mathcal{K}_{n}= \sigma(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n})$
> 2. Given a martingale $X_{n}$ with respect to $\mathcal{G_{n}}$ we get that for any $m > n$ we have $X_{n} = \Exp{X_{m} \midline \mathcal{G}_{n}}$ and also that for any $n, m$ we have $\Exp{X_{n}} = \Exp{X_{m}}$
> 3. Given two martingales $X_{n}$ and $Y_{n}$ with respect to $\mathcal{G}_{n}$ then $X_{n} + Y_{n}$ is a martingale.

`\begin{proof}`

1. Use the fact that for any $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal{G_{n}}$ that $\mathcal{K_{n}} \subset \mathcal{G_{n}}$ which gives us that for any bounded $Z$ with $\sigma(Z) \subset \mathcal{K_{n}}$ gives us $\sigma(Z) \subset \mathcal{G}_{n}$ and thus $\Exp{Z X_{n+1}} = \Exp{Z X_{n}}$ by assumption. This means that $X_{n} = \Exp{X_{n+1} \midline \mathcal{K_{n}}}$
2. Induction
3. Linearity of Conditional Expectation
   `\end{proof}`

## Important Examples

> [!example] Simple Random Walk
> Given i.i.d. random variables $X_{i}$ with $P(X_{i} = 1) = P(X_{i} = -1) = \frac{1}{2}$ for all $i \in \N$ we can define a simple random walk as
> $S_{n} = X_{0} + \sum\limits_{k = 1}^{n} X_{k}$
> where $X_{0} \in \Z$ is simply the starting value.
> This can be visualized as starting at the value $X_{0}$ and moving either left or right depending on a coin flip. The position at the $n$-th step is then described in $S_{n}$.
> This is trivially a Martingale with respect to $\mathcal{F}_{n} = \sigma(X_{1}, \ldots, X_{n})$ most importantly becuase using simple conditional expectation properties we get that
>
> $$
