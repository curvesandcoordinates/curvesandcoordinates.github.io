### Clifford Algebra

> [!definition|\*]
> We call a map $q: M \to R$ a quadratic form if the following properties are satisfies
>
> - $q(\alpha m) = \alpha^{2} q(m)$
> - $\phi: (x, y) \mapsto q(x + y) - q(x) - q(y)$ is bilinear
>
> The _Clifford Algebra_ $\mathrm{Cl}(M, q)$ is the quotient
> $\faktor{T(M)}{\mathcal{J}(q)}$
> where $\mathcal{J}(q) = < x \otimes x - q(x) \mathbb{1} >_{x \in M}$

> [!remark|\*]
>
> - $\mathrm{Cl}(M, 0) = \Lambda(M)$
> - $\phi$ is symmetric i.e. we can swap the entries and $\phi(x, x) = 2 q(x)$, meaning we can also reconstruct the quadratic form from the bilinear form and thus define a clifford algebra given some bilinear form, which is often in in $R = \K$

Now we go through similar properties as we did for the alternating and symmetric algebras. There is some sort of functoriality and universal properties…

---

> [!definition|\*] Lie Algebra
> A Lie Algebra is an $R$-module $L$ with a bilinear map
> $L \times L \to L, (x, y) \mapsto [x, y]$
> such that
>
> 1. $[x, x] = 0$
> 2. $[x, [y, z]] + [y, [z, x]] + [z, [x, y]] = 0$ the Jacobi Relation

> [!definition|\*] Universal Enveloping Algebra
> The universal enveloping algebra $U(L) := \frac{T(L)}{\mathcal{J}}$ with
> $J := < x \otimes y - y \otimes x - [x, y]>_{x, y \in L}$

Similar to before we can observe that if $[x, y] = 0$ meaning a trivial lie bracket, then $U(L) = \mathrm{Sym}(M)$

> [!theorem|\*] Poincare-Birkhof-Witt Theorem
> $\set{e_{i_{1}} e_{i_{2}} \midline i_{1} \leq i_{2} \leq \ldots}$
> is a basis of $U(L)$
