We first focus on a special case of multilinear algebra that should already be known: Linear Algebra which studies for a given Basis linear maps. In this analysis we introduce matrix decompositions, rank, determinants, norms, etc.

In multilinear algebra we consider maps with multiple entries but fixing everything but one entry makes the map linear. Here we can attempt to find similar objects to the ones in linear algebra but the concepts are richer. We also gain a new object, namely what matrices are to linear maps, tensors are to multilinear maps, eventhough the correspondence of representing a multilinear map by tensors is only possible for vector spaces, but we will consider modules. We also connect linear algebra to multilinear algebra via the tensor product.

### Interesting Multilinear Maps

- Determinant
  The determinant is the first interesting multilinear map we come across. Since when identifying $\mathbb{F}^{n\times n} \cong (\mathbb{F}^n)^n$ we can consider the determinant as a map from the coloumns of a matrix
  $M \mapsto \sum\limits_{\pi \in S_{n}} \mathrm{sgn}(\pi) \prod_{i=1}^n M_{i \pi(i)}$
  Sidenote: When removing the sign in this formula we get a map called the permanent, which is very difficult to compute quickly.

- Commutator of Matrices
  Given $(\mathbb{F}^{n\times n})^2 \ni (A, B)$ we can map it to its commutation $[A, B] = AB - BA$. Interestingly the image of the Commutator is a Vector space. Exactly the space of trace 0 matrices. This leads to an open research question: Is the image of a multilinear map always a vector space.

- Standard Polynomial
  $S_{k}: (\mathbb{F}^{n\times n})^k \to \mathbb{F}^{n\times n}, \quad \begin{pmatrix}M_{1} \\ \vdots \\ M_{k}\end{pmatrix} \mapsto \sum\limits_{\pi \in S_{k}} \mathrm{sgn} (\pi) \prod_{j = 1}^k M_{\pi(j)} $
  Which is a generalization of the commutator since for the case of $k = 2$ this is the commutator.

We will talk about a connected theorem: Amitsur-Lewitzki Theorem, which states that $k \geq 2n \implies S_{k}(M) = 0$

- Matrix Multiplication and in the special case also Integer multiplication are multilinear maps.

### Tensor Products & Recursive Constructions

Consider a matrix of matrices, which should be the intuition behind a tensor:
$M_{nm}(\mathbb{F}) \cong M_{n}(M_{m}(\mathbb{F})) \cong M_{n}(\mathbb{F}) \otimes M_{m}(\mathbb{F})$
which turns out to be exactly the tensor product.

A similar thing happens when considering polynomials: Given the space $\mathbb{F}[x]$ and $\mathbb{F}[y]$ we can consider multiplying them, but this will not give us $\mathbb{F}[x, y]$ but will miss some out. What we can do though is a construction that will turn our to be the tensor product
$\mathbb{F}[x] \otimes \mathbb{F}[y] = \span \set{f g \midline f \in \mathbb{F}[x], g \in \mathbb{F}[y]} \cong \mathbb{F}[x, y]$

### Fast recursive algorithms exploiting tensor products

- Matrix multiplication can be optimally done in $O(n^\omega)$ with $\omega < 3$
- Integer multiplication used in Cryptography can be done in $O(n \log(n))$
- Fast Fourier Transform in $O(n \log (n))$
- Quantum Fourier Transform in $O(n \log(n))$

### Tensors in Physics

There are two mainly used definitions of tensors used in Physics

- Multidimensional Array with certain transformation properties (Vague but true)
- Elements of Tensor product spaces (basically all of quantum theory)
