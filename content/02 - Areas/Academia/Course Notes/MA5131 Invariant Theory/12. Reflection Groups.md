Let $V$ be an $n$-dimensional $\K$-Space, $G \subset \mathrm{GL}(V)$

> [!definition|12.1] Reflections and Reflection Groups
> A $\sigma \in G$ is called a reflection if $\dim(V^{\sigma}) = n-1$. Note that $\sigma = \mathrm{id}$ is not a reflection. $G$ is called a reflection group if it is generated by reflections.

> [!remark|\*]
> Let $\sigma$ be a reflection, then the eigenvalue $1$ has geometric multiplicity $n-1$. This implies that
> $\chi_{\sigma}(t) = (t - 1)^{n-1} (t - \lambda), \lambda \in \K$
> Let us consider cases now:
>
> 1. $\lambda \neq 1$ gives us that $\sigma$ is similar to a diagonal matrix $\mathrm{diag}(\lambda, 1, \ldots, 1)$. Also $\mathrm{ord}(\lambda) = \mathrm{ord}(\sigma)$. If additionally if we assume $\K = \R$ we know that $\lambda = -1$ is a classical reflection and for $\lambda \neq \pm 1$ is called a homology
> 2. $\lambda = 1$ gives us that $\sigma$ is similar to $\begin{pmatrix}A & 0 \\ 0 & \mathrm{diag(1, \ldots, 1)}\end{pmatrix}$  with $A =\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1  \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ and called a “transvection”. If the order if is finite this can only happen in positive characteristic.

Let us now consider some examples

> [!example|\*]
>
> - $G = S_{n} \subset \mathrm{GL}_{n}(\K)$ transvections here are transpositions and thus the group is generated by reflections
> - The symmetry group of the regular n-gon is a reflection group.
> - $\mathrm{O}_{n}$ is a reflection group while $\mathrm{SO(n)}$ isnt as the determinants dont match.
> - $\mathrm{GL}(V)$ is a reflection group due to Gauss-Elimination since each of the fundamental steps are either a homology or a transvection. The same is true for $\mathrm{SL}(V)$

==Remember this==

> [!theorem|12.2]
> Let $G$ be a finite reflection group and $\mathrm{char}(\K) = 0$. Then $\K[V]^{G}$ is generated by $n$ invariants. So $\K[V]^{G}$ is isomorphic to a polynomial ring.

`\begin{proof}`

> [!lemma|12.3]
> Let $\sigma \in G$ be a reflection, take $l \in V^{*}$ such that $\at{l}{V^{\sigma}} = 0$ for all $f \in \K[V]$ we have that
> $l \mid (\sigma-1)f$

`\begin{proof}`
Without loss of generality assume that $\K$ is algebraically closed. This means that $\mathcal{I}(V^{\sigma}) = (l)$ where the right is the ideal generated in $\K[V]$. This means we now only need to show that $(\sigma - 1)f \in \mathcal{I}(V^{\sigma})$
thus evaluate for some $v \in V^{\sigma}$ and get
$(\sigma -1)f(v) = f(\sigma^{-1}(v)) - f(v) = 0$
`\end{proof}`

we also need a more technical lemma

> [!lemma|12.4]
> Let $G$ be a finite reflection group $\mathrm{char}(\K) \mid |G|$. Let $f_{1}, \ldots, f_{m} \in \K[V]^{G}$ and $g_{1}, \ldots, g_{m} \in \K[V]$ homogeneous such that
> $\sum\limits_{i = 1}^{m} f_{i} g_{i} = 0$
> Then $f_{1} \in (f_{2, \ldots, f_{m}})_{\K[V]}$ or $g_{i} \in (\K[V]_{+}^{G})_{\K[V]}$ which is the Hilbert Ideal

`\end{proof}`
