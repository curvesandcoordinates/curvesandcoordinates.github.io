> [!definition|12.1] Reflections and Reflection Groups
> A $\sigma \in G$ is called a reflection if $\dim(V^{\sigma}) = n-1$. Note that $\sigma = \mathrm{id}$ is not a reflection. $G$ is called a reflection group if it is generated by reflections.

> [!remark|\*]
> Let $\sigma$ be a reflection, then the eigenvalue $1$ has geometric multiplicity $n-1$. This implies that
> $\chi_{\sigma}(t) = (t - 1)^{n-1} (t - \lambda), \lambda \in \K$
> Let us consider cases now:
>
> 1. $\lambda \neq 1$ gives us that $\sigma$ is similar to a diagonal matrix $\mathrm{diag}(\lambda, 1, \ldots, 1)$. Also $\mathrm{ord}(\lambda) = \mathrm{ord}(\sigma)$. If additionally if we assume $\K = \R$ we know that $\lambda = -1$ is a classical reflection and for $\lambda \neq \pm 1$ is called a homology
> 2. $\lambda = 1$ gives us that $\sigma$ is similar to $\begin{pmatrix}A & 0 \\ 0 & \mathrm{diag(1, \ldots, 1)}\end{pmatrix}$  with $A =\begin{pmatrix}1 & 1  \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ and called a “transvection”. If the order if is finite this can only happen in positive characteristic.

> [!theorem|12.2]
> Let $G$ be a finite reflection group and $\mathrm{char}(\K) = 0$. Then $\K[V]^{G}$ is generated by $n$ invariants. So $\K[V]^{G}$ is isomorphic to a polynomial ring.
