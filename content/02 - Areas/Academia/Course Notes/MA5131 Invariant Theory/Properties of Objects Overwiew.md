# Group / Invariant Ring Property Overview

## No assumptions on G

- No Reynolds operator
- K\[V]^G may not be finitely generated
- Quotient V//G may not exist as affine variety
- HSOP / Molien theory may fail

Example:
G = 𝔾ₐ (additive group)

---

## G finite or linearly reductive

Equivalent:

- G linearly reductive
  ⇔ Reynolds operator exists
  ⇔ K\[V]^G ⊂ K\[V] splits as module

Consequences:

- K\[V]^G finitely generated
- Quotient V//G = Spec(K\[V]^G) exists
- HSOP exists
- Molien formula applies
- Noether bound applies

---

## K\[V]^G finitely generated

Always:

- HSOP f₁,...,f\_r exists
- R finite over K\[f₁,...,f\_r]

Meaning:
V//G → 𝔸ʳ is finite

---

## Cohen–Macaulay (CM)

Equivalent:

- K\[V] free over K\[V]^G
- PHSOP exists
- HSOP behaves like independent variables

Consequences:

- Secondary invariants exist
- Uniform module structure

---

## Polynomial invariant ring

K\[V]^G ≅ K\[f₁,...,f\_r]

Consequences:

- No secondary invariants
- Quotient V//G ≅ 𝔸ʳ
- Smooth quotient

Finite G (char 0):
Polynomial
⇔ G generated by pseudo-reflections
(Chevalley–Shephard–Todd)

---

## Computational consequences

Finite generation:
→ generators exist

Noether bound:
β(G,V) ≤ |G|
→ search generators up to degree |G|

Molien:
H(K\[V]^G,t)
============

1/|G| ∑ 1/det(1 - t g)

→ counts invariants per degree
→ compare with:
(∑ t^{e\_i}) / ∏(1 - t^{d\_i})
to get:

- primary degrees
- secondary degrees
