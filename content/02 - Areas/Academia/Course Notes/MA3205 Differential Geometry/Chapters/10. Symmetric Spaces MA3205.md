# Symmetric Spaces

**Previous**: [[9. Local Symmetry MA3205]]

> [!definition] Covering Maps
> A smooth map $c: M \to N$ is called a covering map iff each point $q \in N$ has a neighbourhood $V$ such that $c$ maps each connected component of $c^{-1}(V)$ diffeomorphically onto $V$. It is called a semi-Riemannian covering map if in addition $c$ is a local isometry.

> [!example]
>
> - The map $c: \R \to S^{1}, t \mapsto e^{it}$ is a covering map but any restriction of it to an interval $I \subset \R$ is not.
> - $SU(2)$ is a double cover of $SO(3)$
> - $\R^{2}$ is a covering of the $2$-dim Torus $T^{2}$

> [!definition] Covers
> A matched covering $(S, \sim)$ of a manifold $M$ is a covering ${} S = \set{U_{\alpha}\midline \alpha \in A} {}$ of $M$ by open sets $U_\alpha$ with relation $\sim$ on $A$, satisfying
>
> 1. $\alpha \sim \alpha$ (reflexive)
> 2. $\alpha \sim \beta \iff \beta \sim \alpha$ (symmetry)
> 3. $\alpha\sim\beta \land \beta \sim \gamma \land U_{\alpha} \cap U_{\beta} \cap U_{\gamma} \neq \emptyset \implies \alpha \sim \gamma$
>    Then $\sim$ is called a matching relation

Given a covering $(S, \sim)$ on $M$ as above define
$\Sigma = \set{(p, a) \in M \times A \midline p \in U_a}$
then $\sim$ is an equivalence relation on $\Sigma$ via
$(p, a) \sim (q, b) \iff p = q \land a \sim b$
Now set ${} M^{*} = \faktor{\Sigma}{\sim} {}$ The maps $\lambda_{a}: U_{a} \to M^{*} \quad p \mapsto [(p, a)]$ are one-to-one and there is a unique way to make $M^{*}$ into a manifold such that each $\lambda_{a}$ is a difffeomorphism onto its image. Now look at $c: M^{*} \to M, \quad [(p, a)] \mapsto p$ is onto and a local diffeomorphism (since $\at{c}{\lambda_{a}(U_{a})} = \lambda_{a}^{-1}$ ). Moreover with $g^{*} = c^{*}(g)$ we turn $M^{*}$ into a semi-Riemannian manifold.
Unfortunately $c$ is not automatically a covering map

> $\phi: M \to N$
> such that $d_{p} \phi = L$

> [!corollary]
> A complete simply connected locally symmetric manifold is symmetric.

`\begin{proof}`
For $p \in T_{p} M$ and $-id = L$ on $T_{p} M$ is a linear isometry that preserves curvature. Apply the theorem gives us a covering map $\phi: M \to M$ with $d_{p} \phi = - id$. Scine $M$ is simply connected $\phi$ is an isometry.
`\end{proof}`

> [!definition] Space Forms
> A space form is a complete connected semi-Riemannian manifold of constant curvature.

> [!proposition] Characterization of Space Forms
> Simply connected space forms are isometric if and only if they have the same dimension index and curvature

`\begin{proof}`
The conditions are boviously necessary. They are sufficient as well since:
Constant curvature implies local symmetry and by above corollary also global symmetry. so simply connected space forms are symmetric. Suppose $M$ and $N$ are two simply connected space forms of the same dimension , index and curvature. This gives us that there are linear isometries between $T_{p} M$ and $T_{q} N$ and the last theorem then implies the existence of a semi-Riemannian covering map which - since we are simply connected - must be an isometry.
`\end{proof}`

> [!cor]
> A complete, simply connected $n$- dimensional Riemannian manifold of constant curvature $C$ is isometric to
>
> - The sphere $S^{n}(r)$ if $C = 1/r^{2}$
> - the euclidean space $\R^{n}$ if $C = 0$
> - the hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^{n}(r)$ if $C = -\frac{1}{r^{2}}$
