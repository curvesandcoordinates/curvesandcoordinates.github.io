# Local Symmetry

**Previous**: [[8. Jacobi Fields MA3205]]
**Next Chapter**: [[10. Symmetric Spaces MA3205]]

> [!definition] Locally Symmetric Spaces
> A semi Riemannian manifold $M$ is called locally symmetric if and only if the covariant differential of the curvature tensor vanishes. Meaning
> $DR = 0$

> [!proposition]
> Let $M$ be a semi-Riemannian manifold. Then the following statements are equivalent
>
> - $DR = 0$ (Local Symmetry)
> - If $X, Y, Z$ are parallel vector fields on a curve $\gamma$. Then $R_{XY} Z$ is also parallel on $\gamma$. (Invariance under Parallel Transport)
> - The sectional curvature is invariant under parallel transport, meaning that given a non-degenerate tangent plane $\Pi$ the sectional curvature remains constant if $\Pi$ is parallel transportet along $\gamma$

`\begin{proof}`

1. $1 \implies 2$
   Fix a point $\gamma(0)$ on $\gamma$ and extend $V = \gamma'(0)$. $X, Y, Z$ to vector fields on a neighbourhood $U$ of $\gamma(0)$. Since $DR = 0$ we have that
   $0 = (D_{V} R)_{XY} Z = D_{V}(R_{XY} Z) - R_{D_{V}(X) Y} Z - R_{X D_{V}(X)} - R_{XY} D_{V}(Z)$ but at $\gamma(0)$ we find that for example $\at{D_{V}(Y)}{\gamma(0)} = (Y_{\gamma})'(0) = Y'(0) = \nabla_{\gamma'}Y = 0$ since $Y$ is parallel. Same holds for $X$ and $Z$ so we are left with
   $(R_{XY}Z)' (0) = D_{\gamma'(0)}(R_{XY}Z) = 0$
   since the point $\gamma(0)$ was arbitrary we know that this holds everywhere meaning $R_{XY} Z$ is parallel to $\gamma$.

2. $2 \implies 1$
   Given $v, x, y, z \in T_{p} M$ and let $X, Y, Z$ be parallel along the geodesic $\gamma_{v}$. Then we know that
   $(D_{V} R)_{XY} Z = (R_{XY} Z)' = 0$

3. $2 \implies 3$
   Let $\Pi$ be a non-degenerate tangent plane at $\gamma(0)$ and let $X, Y$ be parallel vector fields along $\gamma$ that span $\Pi$ at $\gamma(0)$. Then $X(t)$ and $Y(t)$ are a basis for the parallel translate $\Pi(t)$, but then
   $\innprod{R_{XY} X}{Y} \quad \innprod{X}{X} \innprod{Y}{Y} - \innprod{X}{Y}^{2}$ are constant along $\gamma$ and thus is the sectional curvature.
   `\end{proof}`

> [!corollary] Constant Sectional Curvature means local symmetry
> A semi-riemannian Manifold of constant sectional curvature is locally symmetric

> [!definition] Polar Map
> Let $M$ and $N$ be semi-riemannian manifolds of same dimension and index and let $L: T_{p} M \to T_{p} N$ be a linear isometry around $p$. Moreover let $U$ be a normal neighbourhood of $p$ such that $\exp_{q}$ is defined on $L \circ \exp_{p}^{-1}(U)$. Then the mapping
> $\phi_{L} = \exp_{g} \circ L \circ \exp_{p}^{-1}: U \to N$
> is called the **polar map of $L$ on $U$**.

So $\phi_{L}$ sends $\exp_{p}(v)$ to $\exp_{q}(Lv)$ for any $v \in \exp_{p}^{-1}(U) \subset T_{p} M$. For small enough $U$ we always find such a map and the claim is that if it is the differential of some local isometry that isometry must be the polar map

> [!lemma]
>
> 1. $\phi_{L}$ maps radial geodesics to radial geodesics (where we mean geodesics eminating through $p$), meaning if $v \in T_{p} M$ then $\phi_{L}(\gamma_{v}) = \gamma_{Lv}$
> 2. $d_{p} \phi_{L} = L$
> 3. For small enough $U$ we have that $\phi_{L}$ is a diffeomorphism onto a normal Neighbourhood of $q$ in $N$
> 4. If $N$ is complete (meaning $\exp_{q}$ is defined on $T_{q} N$) then $\phi_{L}$ is a diffeomorphism onto a normal neighoburhood of $p$

The first two points give us that if $L$ comes from an isometry then the only candidate is $\phi_L$. Moreover $L$ must preserve local curvature since
$L \left( R_{xy}z \right) = R_{Lx, Ly} Lz$ for all $x, y, z \in T_{p} M$.
Since $\phi_{L}$ is a diffeomorphism we can choose coordinate systems that are preserved. In such a pariting of coordinates a vector field $X$ and its image $d \phi(X)$ will have the same coefficients. Next, we know for an isometry that $g_{M} = \phi^{*}(g_{N})$ which means that the metric tensor components are preserved thus so are the christoffel symbols and thus is the curvature tensor. This means that $L$ preserves sectional curvature. This necessary condition is sufficient in the local symmetric case:

> [!theorem] Locality of Polar Maps
> Let $M$ and $N$ be locally symmetric semi-riemannian manifolds and let $L: T_{p} M \to T_{q} N$ be a linear isometry that preserves curvature. Then:
>
> 1. If $U$ is a sufficiently small normal Neighbourhood of $p$ then there is a unique isometry $\phi$ onto a normal Neighbourhood $V$ of $q$ such that $d_{p} \phi = L$
> 2. If $N$ is complete then for any normal Neighbourhood $U$ of $p$, there exists unique local isometry (meaning a map whose differential is an isometry everywhere) $\phi: U \to N$ such that $d_{p} \phi = L$

> [!definition] Local Geodesic Symmetry
> At any point $p \in M$ let $\zeta_{p}$ be the polar map at $p$ of the linear isometry
> $L: T_{p} M \to T_{p} M, \quad v \mapsto -v$
> For suitable normal neighbourhood $U$ this is a diffeomorphism
> $\zeta_{p}: U \to U$
> Since $\zeta_{p}$ reverses geodesics one finds that $\zeta_{p}(\gamma(s)) = \gamma(-s)$ and $\zeta_{p}$ is called the local geodesic symmetry of $M$ at $p$.

> [!corollary] Local Symmetric Spaces have Local Geodesic Symmetry
> On a semi-riemannian manifold $M$ the following statements are equivalent
>
> 1. $M$ is locally symmetric
> 2. If $L: T_{p} M \to T_{p} M$ is a linear isometry that preserves curvature, there there is an isometry $\phi$ of normal neighbourhoods of $p$ and $q$, such that $d_{p}\phi = L$.
> 3. At each point $p \in M$ the local geodesic symmetry $\zeta_{p}$ is an isometry.

`\begin{proof}`
2\) $\implies$ 2) Follows from the last theorem
3\) $\implies$ 3) $- id$ maps any non-degenerate plane to itself so it preserves sectional curvature. If $\phi_{-id}$ is the isometry given by 2) then it reverses geodesics thus $\zeta_{p} = \phi_{-id}$
4\) $\implies$ 1) Since $\zeta_{p}$ is an isometry it preverses curvature and covariant derivatives. Its differential at $p$ is given by
$d_{p}\zeta_{p} = - id$
thus for all $x, y, z, v \in T_{p} M$ we have
$-(D_{v}R)_{xy}z = (D_{-v}R)_{(-x)(-y)}(-z) = (D_{v}R)_{xy}z$
So $DR = 0$ at $p$. Since $p$ was arbitrary $DR$ vanishes and $M$ is locally symmetric.
`\end{proof}`
