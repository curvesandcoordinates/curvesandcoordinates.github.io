# The Curvature Tensor

**Previous**: [[4.2 Parallel Transport MA3205]]
**Next Chapter**: [[5.2 Sectional and Ricci Curvature MA3205]]

Given that for Lie Derivatives the Jacobi Identity gives us $L_{[X, Y]} = [L_{x}, L_{y}] = L_{X} L_{Y} - L_{Y} L_{X}$
We can measure how much this fails for the covariant derivative by defining the curvature tensor

> [!idea] Intuition via Parallelograms
> We can also think of the curvature tensor as another visual image….

> [!definition] Curvature Tensor
> Let $M$ be a semi-Riemannian manifold then
> $R: \Gamma(TM)^{3} \to \Gamma(TM), \quad R_{XY}Z = \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z - (\nabla_{X} \nabla_{Y} Z - \nabla_{Y} \nabla_{X} Z)$
> is called the curvature tensor of $M$

> [!lemma] Properties of the Curvature Tensor
> $R$ is a $(1, 3)$-tensor field with the following symmetries:
>
> 1. $R_{XY} = -R_{YX}$
> 2. $\innprod{R_{XY}V}{W}= - \innprod{R_{XY} W}{V}$
> 3. $R_{XY} Z + R_{YZ} X + R_{ZX} Y = 0$ (1. Bianchi Identity)
> 4. $\innprod{R_{XY} V}{W} = \innprod{R_{VW} X}{Y}$

`\end{proof}`

Another property is given by the 2. Bianchi Identity

> [!lemma] 2nd Bianchi Identity
> $(\nabla_{Z} R)_{XY} + (\nabla_{X} R)_{YZ} + (\nabla_{Y} R)_{ZX} = 0$

`\begin{proof}`
As always we take constant coefficient continuations of the point values of our vectors $X, Y, Z, V \in T_{p}M$ and then have
$\nabla_{Z} X = \sum\limits_{i} Z^{i} \sum\limits_{k} \left( \del_{i} X^{k} + \sum\limits_{j} \Gamma_{ij}^{k} X^{j}\right) \del_{k} = \sum\limits_{i,j,k} \Gamma_{ij}^{k} X^{j} Z^{i} \del_{k}$
Thus  we can simplify
$R_{\nabla_{Z}X Y} V = \sum\limits_{i,j,k,l} \Gamma_{ij}^{k} X^{j} Z^{i}Y^{l} R_{\del_{k}, \del_{l}}V$
or
$R_{Y \nabla_{X}Z}V = \sum\limits_{i,j,k,l} \Gamma_{ij}^{k} Z^{j}X^{i}Y^{l} R_{\del_{l} \del_{k}} V$

So since $R$ is skey symmetric in the lower slots corresponding terms cancel in the cyclic sum and only terms of the form
$\nabla_{Z} (R_{XY}) - R_{XY} \nabla_{Z} = [\nabla_{Z}, [\nabla_{X}, \nabla_Y]]$
Since the commutator obeys the Jakobi-Identity the cyclic sum is 0
`\end{proof}`

Let us calculate the tensor components of $R$. For historic reasons the naming scheme is the following

$R_{\del_{k} \del_{l}} \del_{j} = \sum\limits_{i} R^{i}_{jkl} \del_{i}$
One gets
$R_{jkl}^{i} = \del_{l} \Gamma_{kj}^{i} - \del_{k} \Gamma_{lj}^{i} - \sum\limits_{m}\Gamma_{lm}^{i} \Gamma^{m}_{kj} - \sum\limits_{m} \Gamma^{i}_{km} \Gamma^{m}_{lj} $
