# Sectional and Ricci Curvature

**Previous**: [[5.2 Sectional and Ricci Curvature MA3205]]

## Sectional Curvature

> [!definition]
> Let $\Pi$ be a non-degenerate $2$-dim plane in $T_{p} M$ and $V, W$ a basis for $\Pi$. Then $k(\Pi) = k(V, W) := \frac{\innprod{R_{VW}V}{W}}{\innprod{V}{V} \innprod{W}{W} - \innprod{V}{W}^{2}}$is called the sectional curvature of $\Pi$

> [!lemma]
> $k(\Pi)$ is independent of the choice of Basis

`\begin{proof}`
Appying a change of basis with the matric $\begin{pmatrix}a  & b  \\ c & d\end{pmatrix}$. Simply inserting gives a common factor of $(ad - bc)^{2}$ in numerator and denomiator.
`\end{proof}`

> [!remark] The sectional curvature is intrinsic to our curvature tensor
> The sectional curvature $k$ determines the curvature tensore uniquely. Meaning that given some tensor we can calculatie the term for the sectional curvature and if these agree with those of our curvature tensor then the given tensor is our curvature tensor.

## Ricci Curvature

> [!definition] Ricci Curvature
> Let $R$ be the curvatur tensor on $M$. The Ricci Curvature is the $(1, 3)$-contraction of the curvature tensor.
> $Ricc = C^{1}_{3}(R)$
> The components are given by
> $R_{ij} = \sum\limits_{m} R^{m}_{ijm}$
> If $\set{E_{i}}$ is an ON-Basis on $T_{p} M$ then
> $Ricc(X, Y) = \sum\limits_{m} \innprod{E_{m}}{E_{m}} \innprod{R_{XE_{m}} Y}{E_{m}}$
> Remember $\innprod{E_{i}}{E_{i}} = \pm 1$. In Particulat basis representation needs that sign as well: $W = \sum\limits_{i} \innprod{E_{i}}{E_{i}} \innprod{W}{E_{i}} E_{i}$
> In particular we get for an ON-Basis
> $Ricc(E_{1}, E_{1}) = \sum\limits_{m} \innprod{E_{m}}{E_{m}} \innprod{R_{E_{1}E_{m}}}{E_{1}E_{m}} = \sum\limits_{m} \innprod{E_{m}}{E_{m}} k(E_{1}, E_{m}) \innprod{E_{1}}{E_{1}}\innprod{E_{m}}{E_{m}} = \innprod{E_{1}}{E_{1}} \sum\limits_{m} k(E_{1}, E_{m})$

> [!remark] Symmetry of Ricci Curvature
> $Ricc$ is symmetric since $R^{i}_{jkl} = R^{l}_{kij}$

---

Given the metric there is a canonical way to convert between $T_{p} M$ and $T_{p} M^{*}$. For any $\omega \in T_{p} M^{*}$ there is exactly one $v \in T_{p} M$ with $\omega(\del_{i}) = \innprod{V}{\del_{i}} = \sum\limits_{j} v^{j} \innprod{\del_{j}}{\del_{i}} = \sum\limits_{j} g_{ij} v^{j}$
Writing $g^{ij}$ for the coefficients of the inverse matrix for $g_{ij}$. Then we find $V^{j}$ as $\sum\limits_{i} g^{ij} \omega(\del_{i})$

> [!definition] Scalar Curvature
> The Scalara Curvature $S$ is the contraction of the Ricci curvature (once on converted a covariant into a contravariant slot via $v \leftrightarrow \innprod{V}{\cdot}$). One finds that
> $S = \sum\limits_{ij} g^{ij} R_{ij}$

Let us now write down the classical differential operators in our setup:

- The gradient of a function $f$ is given by $\innprod{grad f}{V} := df(V)$
- The divergence of a tensor is the contraction of its tensor derivation given by the Levi-Cevita connection. In general that is not unique (one has options for the contraction) but for a vector field $X$ there is only one choice $\div X := C(\nabla X)$
- The Hessian of a function $f$ is $Hess_{f} = \nabla \nabla f$ which is a symmetric bilinear form: $Hess_{f}(X, Y) = \nabla_{Y}(df)(X)= Y(df(X)) - df(\nabla_{Y} X) = YX f - \nabla_{Y}Xf = XY f - \nabla_{X} Y f$ (because $XY f - YX f = (\nabla_{X} Y - \nabla_{Y} X)(f)$)

> [!lemma]
> In a semi-Riemannian manifold $M$ $\frac{1}{2} dS = div Ricc$ holds

`\begin{proof}`
We know the 2nd Biachi identity
$(\nabla_{Z} R)_{XY} + (\nabla_{X} R)_{YZ} + (\nabla_{Y} R)_{ZX} = 0$
holds. Commuting two slots in the third term allows us to rewrite into
$(\nabla_{Z} R)_{XY}(V) + (\nabla_{X} R)_{YZ}(V) = (\nabla_{Y} R)_{XZ} (V)$
Contracting the $Z$ slot with the contravariant one leaves us with
$div \innprod{R_{V \cdot}X}{Y} + \nabla_{X} Ricc(Y, V) = \nabla_{Y} Ricc(X, V)$
Now we contract the $X$ and $V$ slots metrically and finally get the claim
$div Ricc(Y) + div Ricc (Y) = \nabla_{Y}S = dS(Y)$
`\end{proof}`
