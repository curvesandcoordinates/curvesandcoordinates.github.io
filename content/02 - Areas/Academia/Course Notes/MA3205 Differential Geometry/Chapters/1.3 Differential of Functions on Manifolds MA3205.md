# Differential of Functions on Manifolds

**Previous**: [[1.2 Partition of Unity MA3205]]
**Next Chapter**: [[2.1 Vector Bundles and the Tangential Bundle MA3205]]

Applying this to our mainfolds and tangent spaces, consider a smooth map $f: \R^{n} \to \R^{m}$. We know that $D_{p} f: \R^{n} \to \R^{m}$ is a linear map. We would like to find a version of this total differential at a point in the language of manifolds. Since $T_{p} M$ is already a vector space this would be a linear map aswell

> [!definition] Differential of a function on manifolds
> Let $\psi: M \to N$ be a smooth map. The differential of $\psi$ in $p \in M$ is the linear map
> $d_{p} \psi : T_{p}M \to T_{\psi(p)} N$
> for which for all $v \in T_{p} M$ and $f \in \mathcal{F}(N)$ we have
> $ d_{p} \psi(v) (f) = v(f \circ \psi)$

We have to check the well-definedness of this map, meaning is $d_{p}\psi(v) \in T_{\psi(p)} N$. $\R$-Linearity is clearly given, meaning we only have to check the leibniz property.

$$
\begin{align*}
d_{p}\psi(v)(fg) &= v((fg) \circ \psi) = v((f \circ \psi) (g \circ \psi)) \\
&= v(f \circ \psi) g(\psi(p)) + f(\psi(p)) v(g \circ \psi) \\
&= d_{p}\psi(v)(f) g(\psi(p)) + g(\psi(p)) d_{p}\psi(v)(g) \end{align*}
$$

> [!example]
>
> - Let $f: \R^{n} \to \R$ be  a smooth function. Given $v \in \R^{n}$ we can consider the directional derivative $\at{\del_{v}(\cdot)}{p}: f \mapsto <grad f|p, v>$. So here we identify $T_{p}\R^{n}$ with $\R^{n}$. If we are given $g: \R^{n} \to \R^{m}$ then considering the the total differential $D_{p}g: \R^{n} \to \R^{m}$ is a linear map. With the above identification we can view this as a map we can see that $D_{p}g = d_{p}g$ since
>
> $$\begin{align*}d_{p}g(v)(f) &= v(f \circ g) \
> $$

&= \innprod{grad (f \circ g)}{v} \\
&= D\_{p}(f\circ g)(v) = D\_{g(p)}(f(D\_{p}g(v)) \\
&= \innprod{grad f|_{g(p)}}{D_{p}g (v)} \\
&= D\_{p}g(v)(f)\end{align\*}\$\$

> - For a given open Interval $I \subset \R$ and $M$ a manifold we call a smooth map $\gamma: I \to M$ a curve on M. We can then consider the directional derivative of $\gamma$ as: $\del_{1} \gamma |_{t_{0}} = \frac{d}{dt} \gamma(t)|_{t_{0}} \in T_{\gamma(t_{0})M}$ For any smooth function $f \in \mathcal{F}(M)$ we can form the map $f \circ \gamma: I \to \R$ and get $\frac{d}{dt} (f \circ \gamma)|_{t_{0}} = d_{\gamma(t_{0})}f(\del_{1} \gamma |_{t_{0}})$.
> - Consider the open submanifold $GL(n, \R) = \set{A \in \R^{n \times n} \midline \det(A) \neq 0} \subset \R^{n^{2}}$. The determinant map on this manifold is a smooth map $\det: GL(n, \R) \to \R$. We also know that $\det(E + tB) = 1 + t \, tr(B) + O(t^{2})$meaning $\frac{d}{dt} \det(E+ tB) = tr(B)$.

The last example simply assumed a manifold as it is an open subset of an already known manifold. In general we can consider $f: M \to \R$ smooth with $df \neq 0$. Then the set $N = \set{p \in M \midline f(p) = c}$ for some $c \in \R$ is a (sub-)manifold of $M$ by the implicit function theorem, since $N$ can locally be written as a graph. The dimension of $N$ is given by $\dim N = \dim M - 1$.

> [!example]
> Consider the matricies $SL(n, \R) = \set{A \in GL(n, \R) \midline det A = 1}$ is a $n^{2} - 1$ dimensional manifold.

In a more general way we can state the following

> [!definition] Submanifolds
> Let $M$ be a Manifold. A manifold $N \subset M$ is called a submanifold of $M$ if $N$ is a topological subspace (with the induced topology), the inclusion map $i: N \to M$ is smooth and $di$ is injective.

> [!example]
>
> - Open submanifolds are submanifolds.
> - $T^{2} \subset \R^{3}$ is a submanifold.
> - $S^{n} \subset \R^{n+1}$ are submanifolds.
