# Sub-bundles, Submersions, Foliations and Frobenius Theorem

**Previous**: [[2.3 Baby Lie Groups MA3205]]
**Next Chapter**: [[3. Tensors "in a nutshell" MA3205]]

> [!definition] Subbundle
> A $p$-Subbundle $F$ of a $k$-vector bundle $E$ over $M$ is a topological subspace of $E$ that is a $p$-vector bundle over $M$ such that each fiber of $F$ is a $p$-subspace of the corresponding fiber of $E$. A subbundle of the tangential bundle is sometimes called a distribution.

![[04 - Resources/Assets/640px-Subbundle.png]]

> [!example]
> A non-vanishing vector field defines a $1$-dim. distribution, by at each point taking the span of the corresponding tangent vector. Its maximal integral curves foliate the manifold **locally** into 1-dimensional submanifolds.

> [!definition]
> A $p$-folitation of a manifold $M$ is a partition $P = \set{P_{i}}$ of $M$ into path-connected sets called its leaves. This construction has to be such that there is an atlas for $M$ for which the charts $\phi: U \to \R^{n}$ map non-emtpy connected components of $U \cap P_{i}$ into $p$-planes in $\R^{n}$.

> [!remark]
> Alternatively one can define a foliation as an atlas such that for any two charts: $\phi: U \to \R^n$ and $\eta: V \to \R^{n}$ the transition functions $\phi \circ \eta^{-1}$ factorize $\phi \circ \eta^{-1}(u, v) = (f_{1}(u), f_{2}(u, v))$ with $f_{1}: \R^{n-p} \to \R^{n-p}$ and $f_{2}(u, \cdot): \R^{p} \to \R^{p}$

> [!example] Level-Sets on Manifolds
> Now let us consider an example. Let $M$ and $N$ be manifolds with $\dim M > \dim N$ and $f: M \to N$ a submersion (meaning a smooth map with surjective differential). Then the implicit function theorem implies that the level sets of f form a foliation.

> [!theorem] Frobenius Theorem
> A $k$-distribution $D$ is integrable (or involutive - meaning $X, Y \in \Gamma(D) \implies [X, Y] \in \Gamma(D)$) if and only if it comes from a foliation (meaning its fibers are tangent to the leaves of the foliation).

Mentally one should consider this theorem as answering the question that given a function $f$ and we consider $\del_{x} f = \del_{y} f$ does this mean that $\del_{xy} f = \del_{yx} f$?

`\begin{proof}`
Of course the condition to be an involution is necessary. The leaves of a foliation are locally submanifolds and the Lie bracket of tangential vector fields need to be tangential vector fields again.

So we only need to show sufficiency. For this our remark gives that it is enough to find at any point a coordinate system such that the distribution is spanned by the first $\set{\del_{1}, \cdots, \del_{k}}$.
First we see that there is a local basis $X_{1}, \cdots, X_{k}$  for the distribution $D$ with $X_{i}= \del_{i} + \sum\limits_{j = k+1}^{n} a_{ij} \del_{j}$
Given a coordinate system and a local basis $Y_{1}, \cdots, Y_{k}$ we know that $Y_{i} = \sum\limits_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} \del_{j}$ and the matrix $(a_{ij})$ has rank $k$ and since the $Y_{i}$ form a basis, we can assume that the $k \times k$ submatrix is not singular. Denote its inverse by $(c_{ij})$. Now set $X_{i}= \sum\limits_{j=1}^{k} c_{ij} Y_{j}$ then basically rotating the coordinates of $Y$ in such a way that the first k elements are trivial.
Since our distribution is involutive we can show that $[X_{i}, X_{j}] = 0$ since
$[X_{i}, X_{j}] \in \span(X_{1}, \cdots X_{k}) \overset{\text{since } [\del_{i}, \del_{j}] = 0}{\implies} [X_{i}, X_{j}] \in \span(\del_{k+1}, \cdots, \del_{n})$
and since thus $[X_{i}, X_{j}] \in \span(X_{1}, \cdots X_{k}) \cap \span(\del_{k+1}, \cdots, \del_{n}) = \set{0}$ so the bracket must vanish.

Finally we need to still show that this allows for a coordinate system with $\del_{i} = X_{i}$. We have done this in the case of $k = 1$ in the proof of [[2.1 Vector Bundles and the Tangential Bundle MA3205|Vector Bundles]]. Thus simply do induction over $k$, meaning we assume we have a local coordinate system $\phi = (x_{1}, \cdots, x_{n})$ with $\del_{i} = X_{i}$ for $i \in \set{1, \cdots, k-1}$.

Write $X_{k} = \sum\limits_{j=1}^{n} a_{j}\del_{j}$. Since $[X_{i}, X_{j}] = 0$ we can wrtie $0 = [X_{k}, \del_{i}] = \sum\limits_{j} [a_{j} \del_{j}, \del_{i}] = \sum\limits_{j}a_{j}[\del_{j}, \del_{i}]- (\del_{i} a_{j}) \del_{j}$
Where the last equality is from the non $\mathcal{F}$ linearity of the Lie bracket shown before. The Right term simplifies to
$0 = \sum\limits_{j} (\del_{i} a_{j}) \del_{j}$
and since $\del_{j}$ form a basis we know that $\del_{i} a_{j} = 0$ which holds for $i \in \set{1, \cdots, k-1}$. This means that $a_{j}$ is independent of the first $k-1$ coordinates. Now setting $Y = X_{k} - \sum\limits_{j=1}^{k-1} a_{j}\del_{j} = \sum\limits_{j=k}^{n} a_{j} \del_{j}$ ensures that $Y$ depends only on the last $n-k$ coordinates. Keep in mind that $\Span(X_{1}, \cdots X_{k}) = \Span(\del_{1}, \cdots, \del_{k-1}, Y)$
Now we can change our coordinate system $\eta = (y_{1}, \cdots y_{n})$ so that the first $k-1$ coordinates stay the same so $\frac{\del}{\del x_{i}} = \frac{\del}{\del y_{i}} \quad 1 \leq i < k$ and $\frac{\del}{\del y_{k}} = Y = \frac{\del}{\del x_{k}} + \sum\limits_{j=k+1}^{n} b_{j} \frac{\del}{\del x_{j}}$`\end{proof}`

> [!example]
> Let $G$ be a group and $H$ a subgroup of $G$. The left co-sets of $H$ are the sets $gH = \set{g h \in G \midline h \in H}$. Now let $G$ be a Lie group and $\mathcal{h}$ is a closed sub Lie algebra of its Lie algebra $\mathcal{g}$. The left invariant vector fields of $\mathcal{h}$ form an integrable subbundle. The Image of $\mathcal{h}$ under the exponential map generates a Lie subgroup $H$ of $G$ with $\mathcal{h}$ as its Lie algebra.
> Then the co-sets of $H$ are the leaves of the foliation generated by $h$, since the leaf through a point $g \in G$ is $gH$.
