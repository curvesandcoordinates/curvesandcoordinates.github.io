The language in which the previous local similarity of $SU(n)$ and the sphere product is expressed is that of [[De Rahm Cohomology|de Rahm cohomology]], which is based on the general theory of [[Differential Forms on Manifolds|differential forms on manifolds]]. Differential forms are an algebraic extension of functions on manifolds, that generalise objects on which we would typically integrate over. These integrable objects on a manifold reveal some intrinsic information of the underlying manifold in the form of cohomology groups.

Visually speaking, we can consider a $0$-Form to simply be a real valued function on a manifold. A $1$-Form in turn measures how much tangential vectors on a manifold point in a specified direction. Higher-order forms generalise this measurement to oriented parallelepipeds on manifolds. It will also become useful to relate $k$-Forms and $k+1$-Forms. We will do this via the exterior derivative, which in some sense checks how much a $k$-Form changes locally and constructs a $k+1$-Form by considering the local change and the original value of our form at a point. Geometric information is then found when considering so-called closed $k$-Forms and seeing which of these aren't "trivially closed". A more in-depth look on differential forms can be seen in \[@bott] or \[@lee\_2012].
