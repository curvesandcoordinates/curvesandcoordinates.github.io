We begin by considering the simple 2D Case of differential geometry, given by planar curves. We define [[Discrete Planar Curves]] and give multiple examples of such curves

- [[Discrete Cycloid]]
- [[Discrete Logarithmic Spiral]]

For these objects we require the study of tangential vectors. In the discrete case this concept cant be given by a derivative and thus can be approximated in multiplie different useful ways.

Firstly we consider the [[Tangent Vectors of Discrete Planar Curves|Edge-Tangent Vector]] which captures simply the direction of each edge on a curve given its points. This does not, at every vertex point tangentially along the curve though which is why using the harmonic mean we can define a second vector, namely the [[Tangent Vectors of Discrete Planar Curves|Vertex Tangent Vector]].

We then note that using the Edge-Tangent Vector we can define properties such as discrete Arc-Length and conditions under which our curves are arc-length parametrized and for that matter sufficiently regular. Having defined arc-length parametrization we aim to discretize the theory of changing parametrizations since here we can see that given a discrete set of points there is no natural way to consider reparametrizations.

We achieve this theory by considering reparametrizations of a curve (in the smooth setting) as applications of a [[Tangential Flow|tangential flow]] given on a curve. To discretize this we consider [[Tangential Flow on Discrete Curves]] and use this instead of explicit reparametrization.

We then go into Curvature and notice that also here we have multiple ways to define [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves]] by either [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves#Vertex Osculating Circles|Vertex Osculating Circles]] or [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves#Edge-Osculating Circles|Edge-Osculating Circles]]. Both of these have their limits which is why we choose the middle path by defining [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves#Curvature for Arc-Length Parametrized Discrete Curves|Curvature for Arc-Length Parametrized Discrete Curves]].

We can then consider the interaction of our “reparametrizing” tangential flow onto our curvature. In the continous case we have that $\dot \kappa = \kappa'$ meaning that curvature is transported along tangential flows. A similar thing is found by considering [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves#Curvature Evolution under Tangential Flow]] and fining that
$\frac{\dot \kappa}{1 + \frac{\kappa}{4}} = \alpha \Delta\kappa$

which is a transport equation that simply states that the curvature of a single vertex under the action of a tangential flow changes according to the curvature around it. Meaning the shape under tangential flow essentially stays the same which justifies it as a replacement for our reparametrizations.

Another justification for our formulae are given by considering another avenue to define curvature, given by considering transport of our curves by some choice of Normal vector given in [[Curvature of Discrete Planar Curves#Curvature Via Normals]].

## Curves in the Complex Projective Line

We now want to consider curves
$\gamma: \Z \to \mathbb{CP}^{1}$and consider what quantities of these curves are invariants under [[Möbius Transformations]].  We then consider an invariant of Möbius transformations given by four specified points which is namely the [[Cross-Ratio]].

Going back to curves we can now consider the Cross-Ratio of four consequtive points on our curve.

Consider four points on our curve $\gamma$ and you will see that

$$$\begin{align*}
\mathrm{cr}(\gamma_{\bar{1}}, \gamma, \gamma_{11}, \gamma_{1}) &= \frac{(\gamma_{\bar{1}} - \gamma)(\gamma_{11} - \gamma_{1})}{(\gamma - \gamma_{11}) (\gamma_{1} - \gamma_{\bar{1}})} \\
&= \frac{(-\Delta \gamma_{\bar{1}}) \Delta\gamma_{1}}{-(\Delta\gamma_{1} + \Delta\gamma)(\Delta\gamma + \Delta\gamma_{\bar{1}})} \\
&= \frac{\Delta \gamma_{\bar{1}} \Delta\gamma_{1}}{(\Delta\gamma_{1} + \Delta\gamma)(\Delta\gamma + \Delta\gamma_{\bar{1}})} \\
&= \frac{\Delta \gamma_{\bar{1}}}{\Delta\gamma + \Delta\gamma_{\bar{1}}} \cdot \frac{\Delta \gamma_{1}}{\Delta\gamma + \Delta\gamma_{1}}\\
&= \left( \left(1 + \frac{\Delta\gamma}{\Delta \gamma_{\bar{1}}}\right) \cdot \left(1 + \frac{\Delta\gamma}{\Delta \gamma_{1}}\right) \right)^{-1}\\
\end{align*}$$
Using the formula that
$$\frac{2i+\kappa}{2i - \kappa} = \frac{\Delta\gamma}{\Delta\gamma_{\bar{1}}}$$ we get
$$\mathrm{cr}(\gamma_{\bar{1}}, \gamma, \gamma_{11}, \gamma_{1}) = \frac{1}{16}\left(2 i (\kappa_{1} - \kappa) + \kappa \kappa_{1} + 4\right)$$
which gives us an intersting formula for the [[Schwarzian Derivative]]. This derivative measures how close it is to a Möbius transformation and can thus be seen as a “projective curvature”.

We then consider [[Flows on Discrete Curves on the Complex Projective Line]] by brining a given curve $\gamma: I \to \mathbb{CP}^{1}$ to its family of embeddings $\Gamma: I \to \C^{2}$ which we can choose one canonically. Using this we can consider the Schwarzian derivative (or more precicely the Cross-Ratio of consecutive points) as a measure of the curve and characterize Flows by how they change this measure which will be similar to the Transport Equation in the Planar case.

We then go towards defining transformations of our discrete curve, by prescribing a specific Cross-Ratio and keeping this constant. This is then called the [[Discrete Darboux Transform]] of a Curve and we will consider the Eucleadean version of it asweel, leading to the Tractrix.
