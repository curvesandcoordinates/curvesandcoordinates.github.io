We have to quickly define the basics of [[Groups|group theory]] to work on analysis. The main result here is that we will be mainly be working on the group $\Z_{n}$ which is in turn isomorphic to any finite [[Cyclic Groups|cyclic group]]. If we consider general finite groups we can referr to the [[Structure Theorem for Finite Abelian Groups|structure theorem for finite abelian groups]] which tells us that for any finite abelian group $G$
$G \cong \Z_{r_{1}} \times \Z_{r_{2}} \times \ldots \times \Z_{r_{k}}$
with specific $\set{r_{i}}_{i=1}^{k}$

We then define the [[Group Characters|dual to a group]] by explaining what a kind of “group functional” is. We call this functional a [[Group Characters|character]] and use the motivation to prove that for finite groups just like finite dimensional vector spaces the dual becomes simply isomorphic to the group. This in turn is used in Fourier analysis on finite groups to transform a function.
