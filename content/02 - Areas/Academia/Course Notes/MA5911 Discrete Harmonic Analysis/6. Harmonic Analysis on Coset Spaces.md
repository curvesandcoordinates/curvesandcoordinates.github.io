## 6.1 Group Actions and Homogeneous Spaces

Let $G$ be a (finite) group, $X$ a (finite) Set. Then we call a mapping
$G \times X \to X, \quad (g, x) \mapsto g \cdot x$
with the properties that

$$$\begin{align}
e \cdot x &= x  \\
(gg') \cdot x &= g \cdot (g' \cdot x)
\end{align}$$
This construct has some trivial example that sadly is not finite but nevertheless makes sense

$G = SO(n)$ and $X = \R^n$ then the natural group action is given by the matrix multiplication

This construct is called a $G$-left action of $G$ which we will always implicitly assume

Now we define
$$\mathcal{J}(X) = \set{\phi: X \to X \midline \phi \text{{ bijective}}}$$
then Clearly $\mathcal{J}(X)$ is a group with respect to composition. If $G$ acts on $X$ from the left then we can see that for
$$\phi_{g}: X \to X, \quad \phi_{g}(X) = g \cdot x$$
we have $\phi_{g} \in \mathcal{J}(X)$.
We can now naturally define a group homomorphism
$$\rho: G \to \mathcal{J}(X), \quad \rho(g) = \phi_{g}$$

The action of $G$ on $X$ is called faithfull if $\rho$ is injective

> [!example|6.1]
> - Trivially every group $G$ acts on itself. The action is also trivially faithful.
> - If $H < G$ then $H$ acts on $G$ via $$H \times G \to G, \quad (h, g) \mapsto hg$$ eventhough the other way $G \times H \to H$ is in general not an action.
> - Let $H < G$ and $X = \faktor{G}{H}$. Then $$G \times X \to X, \quad (g, uH) \mapsto (gu) H$$is an action of $G$ onto the Coset space $X$ which is not faithful
> - Let $H, K < G$ and for $g \in G$ let $$HgK = \set{hgk \midline h \in H, k \in K}$$ This is an $(H,K)$ coset. We now write $H \ G / K$  for the set of $(H,K)$ cosets. If $H = K$ we write $\frac{G/}{/}K$ and call it a double coset space.
>Note that $g \sim g' \iff HgK = Hg'K$ is an equiavlece realtion, hence we can decompose $G$ since
>$$HgK \cap Hg'K = \emptyset \iff g \neq g'$$ thus giving us  that $$G = \bigcup_{g \in G} HgK$$
>The canonical projection
>$$q_{H}^K: G \to \frac{H\G}{K}, g \mapsto HgK$$
>the group $G$ acts on $\frac{H\G}{K}$ via 
>$$(g, HuK) \mapsto H(gu)K$$
>this action is in general not faithful
>- $G$ acts on itsself by conjugation $$G \times G \to G, (g, u) \mapsto gug^{-1}$$ more generally let $X = \set{H \midline H < G}$ then $(g, H) \mapsto gHg^{-1}$ is also an action
>- The $(\pi, V)$ be a representation of $G$ then $$G \times V \to V, (g, v) \mapsto \pi(g)v$$ is an action. COnversely every action of $G$ on a linear space gives a representation
>- Let $G$ be a finite group, $X$ a finite set acted on by $G$ then $$G \times \C^X \to \C^X, \quad (g, f) \mapsto g \cdot f$$with $(g \cdot f)(x) = f(gx)$

Let $G$ act on $X$, $S \subset X$ is called $G$-incariant if $g \cdot s \in S$ for every $s \in S, g \in G$.

For $x \in X$ the set
$$Gx = \set{g \cdot x \midline g \in G}$$
is called the G-orbit of $x \in X'$

We can determine that orbits form a equivalence reltion on $X$ by
$$x \sim y \iff Gx = Gy$$
Hence $$Gx \cap Gy = \emptyset \iff x \neq y, X = \bigcup_{x \in X} Gx$$
We call an action of $G$ onto $X$ transitive if there exists an $x \in X$ such that
$$Gx = X$$
In this case $X$ is called a Homogeneous space.

Importantly all homogeneous spaces appear as group quotients, which allows us to always study group quotients

## 6.2 Invariant Functions

Let $G$ be a (finite) group, $K \leq G$ and set
$$X_{K} = \faktor{G}{K}, \quad _{K}X = \lfaktor{K}{G} $$
A function $f \in \C^G$ is called right or respectively left invariant with respect to $K$ if either
$$f(gK) = f(g) \quad f(Kg) = f(g)$$

Let $$\C_{K}^G = \set{f \in \C^G \midline f(gK) = f(g)}$$ and $$_{K}\C^G = \set{f \in \C^G \midline f(Kg) = f(g)}$$
We can simply note that $_{K} \C^G, \C_{K}^G$ are subalgebras of $(\C^G, \cdot)$ with the point-wise product. Then we can consider a mapping
$$\C^{X_{K}} \to \C_{K}^G, \quad f^z \mapsto f = f^z \circ p_{K}$$
and 
$$\C^{_{K}X} \to _{K}\C^G, \quad ^zf \mapsto f = ^zf \circ _{k}p$$

Consider the example of the sphere. This tells you that considering functions invariant under rotations of $SO(n-1)$ amounts to consider functions on the $S^n-1$ sphere…

### Projectors
We can define a map
$$\mathcal{P}_{K}: \C^G \to \C_{K}^G, \quad \mathcal{P}_{K} f = f_{k}$$ where $f_{k}(g) = \frac{1}{|K|} \sum\limits_{k \in K} f(gk)$

and analogously 
$$_{K}\mathcal{P}: \C^G \to _{K}\C^G, \quad _{K}\mathcal{P} f = _{K}f$$ where $_{K}f(g) = \frac{1}{|K|} \sum\limits_{k \in K} f(kg)$

These projectors are othrogonal projectors in $L^2(G)$ since
$$\innprod{\mathcal{P}_{K} f}{g} = \innprod{f}{\mathcal{P}_{K} g}$$
then 
$$\innprod{(I-\mathcal{P}_{K}) f }{ \mathcal{P}_{K} f} = \innprod{f}{\mathcal{P}_{K} f} - \innprod{f}{\mathcal{P}^2_{K} f} = 0$$

We can then similarly write down the set of all bi-invariant functions and projectors etc.
