Talking to Tina we began by talking about tensors and her primary examples were stress-tensors. She gave me some intuition after I tried to explain the intuition behind topology and my BA Thesis. There is apparently a central number that calculates the Stress-Strain curve of an object.

While trying to understand I said that you would take some object, say carbon fiber object with different layering of and orientation of the stand and calculate the stress-strain curve. This tells you via strain how much the applied stress deforms the object inelastically.

She told me about a lab where they did exactly that and I got to asking if the lab felt like she learnt something or did she just give correct hypothesis and not really get info.

This led into a discussion of the following points

- Always being Correct in your Hypothesis tells us that the model is very good, but it doesnt make you learn anything new
- The other direction means that only failed experiements teach.
- When you do the experiements, no matter the hypothesis you learn how the material behaves in the real world.

This feels like Mary’s Room, which which the question of Qualia is brought up. The way Tina makes it sound is that there is a certain information contained in experiemntation that you obtain even if everything happens as expected.

To me this feels like we have a model that approximates reality and the “qualia” is simply just the affirmation that the model is true to the 1001th degree instead of just the 1000th degree.

I guess mainly if feels like a waste of time to know the theory and know it is mostly correct and predict something and pretend it could be wrong.
