> [!definition|\*] Symmetric Algebra
> The symmetric algebra $S(M) = \faktor{T(M)}{J}$ of an $R$-module $M$ is the $R$-algebra obtained as the quotient of $T(M)$ by the ideal $J$ generated by $\set{m_{1} \otimes m_{2} - m_{2} \otimes m_{1} \midline m_{1}, m_{2} \in M}$

This ideal generation here is always in the context of [[Algebras|algebras]], meaning
$J = \set{\sum\limits_{k = 1}^{n} a_{k} b_{k} c_{k} \midline n \in \N, b_{k} \in \text{our set}, a_{k}, c_{k} \in T(M)} = \bigoplus_{n \in \N_{0}} J_{n}$
We c an also write $J_{n} = \mathrm{span}_{R}\set{x \otimes (m_{1} \otimes m_{2} - m_{2} \otimes m_{1}) \otimes y \midline k + l = n -1, x \in M^{\otimes k}, y \in M^{\otimes l}, m_{i} \in M}$ with $J_{0} = J_{1} = 0$

> [!corollary|\*]
>
> 1. $S(M)$ is commutative
> 2. $S(M) = \bigoplus_{n \in \N} S^{n}(M)$ with $S^{n}(M) := \faktor{M^{\otimes n}}{J_{n}}$ the symmetric power of $M$ and $S^{0}(M) = R$ and $S^{1}(M) = M, S^{n}(M) = ...$

`\begin{proof}`
For the first statement note that the multiplcation in $S(M)$ is given by
$(m + J) \cdot (m' + J) = m \otimes m' + J$
therefore the quotient forces commutativity since
$(a + J) (b + J) - (b + J)(a + J) = (a \otimes b - b \otimes a) + J = J$
The second statement is obtained by noting that $J$ is generated by homogeneous order $2$ tensors, thus $J_{n} = 0$ for $n < 2$ so $S^{0}(M) = R$ and $S^{1}(M) = M$.
For $n \geq 2$ elements of $J_{n}$ are finite sums of tensors ….
`\end{proof}`

> [!proposition|\*] Universal Property for maps into commutative algebras
> For any associative commutative $R$-Algebra $A$ and $R$-Module Homomorphism $\phi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M, A)$ there is a unique $R$-Algebra homomorphism $\psi: S(M) \to A$ such that
> $ \begin{CD} M   @>\phi>> A \\ @V\iota_{1} VV @AA\psi A \\  T(M) @> \pi>> S(M)\end{CD}$
> commutes

`\begin{proof}`
We know that there is an $R$-algebra homomorphism $\tilde{\psi}: T(M) \to A$ such that $\phi = \tilde{\psi} \circ \iota_{1}$. For this comutativity of $A$ implies that
$\at{\tilde{\psi}}{J} = 0$
Meaning extending the map $\tilde{\psi}$ to some map
$\psi: S(M) \to A$
works.
`\end{proof}`

> [!proposition|\*] Functoriality Property for maps into commutative algebras
> For any associative commutative $R$-Algebra $N$ and $R$-Module Homomorphism $\phi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M, N)$ there is a unique $R$-Algebra homomorphism $\psi: S(M) \to S(N)$ such that
> $ \begin{CD} M   @>\phi>> N \\ @V\iota_{1} VV @VV\iota_{1} V \\  S(M) @> \pi>> S(N)\end{CD}$
> commutes

`\begin{proof}`
This follows from the functoriality of the Tensor algebra.
`\end{proof}`

> [!definition|\*] Symmetric and Anti-Symmetric Multilinear Maps
> Let $M, N$ be $R$-modules and $f: M^{n} \to N$ multilinear.
> The map is called **symmetric** if
> $f( m_{\sigma(1)}, \ldots, m_{\sigma(n)}) = f(m_{1}, \ldots, m_{n})$
> for any $\sigma \in S_{n}$.
> The map is called **anti-symmetric** if
> $f( m_{\sigma(1)}, \ldots, m_{\sigma(n)}) = \sgn(\sigma)  \cdot f(m_{1}, \ldots, m_{n})$
> for any $\sigma \in S_{n}$.
> The map is called **alternating** if
> $m_{i} = m_{j} \implies f(m_{1}, \ldots, m_{n}) = 0$
> for any $i \neq j$

In the literature there is a reasonable equivalence of alternating and anti-symmetric maps due to the following equivalences

> [!corollary|\*] Equivalence of Anti-Symmetric and Alternating maps
>
> 1. $\text{alternating} \implies \text{anti-symmetric}$
> 2. $\text{anti-symmetric} \implies \text{alternating}$ for $2 \in R^{\times}$

`\begin{proof}`
We will sketch the proof for $n = 2$

For the first consider that
$0 = f(a + b, a + b) = f(a, a) + f(b, b) + f(a, b) + f(b, a) = f(a, b) + f(b, a) \implies \text{anti-symmetry}$
Conversely consider
$f(m, m) = - f(m, m) \implies 2 f(m, m) = 0$
then this only implies $f(m, m) = 0$ if $2 \in R^{\times}$.
`\end{proof}`

> [!proposition|\*] Universality of Multilinear Maps in Tensor Algebras
> For any associative commutative $R$-Algebra $N$ and $R$-Module symmetric multilinear map $\phi \in \mathrm{Hom}_{R}(M^{n}, N)$ there is a unique linear map $\Phi: M^{\otimes n} \to S^{n}(M)$ such that
> $ \begin{CD} M^{n}   @>\phi>> N \\ @V\iota_{n} VV @AA\Phi A\\  M^{\otimes n} @> \pi>> S^{n}(M)\end{CD}$
> commutes

`\begin{proof}`
We know from the univeral property of the tensor product that there exists $\tilde{\Phi}: M^{\otimes n} \to N$ such that
$\tilde{\Phi}(m_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes m_{n}) = \phi(m_{1}, \ldots, m_{n})$
but due to symmetry of $\phi$ we know that $\tilde{\Phi}(J_{n}) = 0$. Thus the map passes to a map $\Phi: S^{n}(M) \to N$
`\end{proof}`

> [!definition|\*]
>
> - For any $\sigma \in S_{n}$ define $\widehat{\sigma} \in \mathrm{End}_{R}(M^{\otimes n})$ such that on elementary tensors it acts as
>   $\widehat{\sigma}(m_{1} \otimes \ldots \otimes m_{n}) := m_{\sigma^{-1}(1)} \otimes \ldots \otimes m_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}$
> - $t \in M^{\otimes n}$ is called symmetric if $\widehat{\sigma}(t) = t$ and anti-symmetric if $\widehat{\sigma}(t) = \sgn(\sigma) t$.
> - Let $S_{n}(M)$ be the symmetric tensors in $M^{\otimes n}$
> - Let $\Lambda_{n}(M)$ be the anti-symmetric tensors in $M^{\otimes n}$

The overload of notation of the letter $S$ is annoying but will be solved a little when proving that the objects $S_{n}(M)$ and $S^{n}(M)$ are isomorphic algebras.

> [!proposition|\*] Symmetrization Map
> Suppose $n! \in R^{\times}$. The map $\mathrm{sym}: M^{\otimes n} \to S_{n}(M), t \mapsto \frac{1}{n!} \sum_{\sigma \in S_{n}} \widehat{\sigma}(t)$
> is a surjective projection (meaning its idempotent) onto $S_{n}(M)$ and $\ker(\mathrm{sym}) = J_{n}$

`\begin{proof}`
For $t \in S_{n}(M)$ we get that
$\mathrm{sym}(t) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} \widehat{\sigma}(t) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} t = t$
and thus $\mathrm{sym}$ is a surjective projection.

We know that $J_{n} = \mathrm{span}_{R}\set{t - \widehat{\sigma}(t) \midline t \in M^{\otimes n}}$ eventhough we wrote it differently before. From this we know that acting on $J_{n}$ by $\mathrm{sym}$ maps to $0$ and thus
$J_{n} \subset \ker(\mathrm{sym})$
Conversely for any $t \in \ker(\mathrm{sym})$ we know that
$t = t - \mathrm{sym}(t) = t - \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} \widehat{\sigma}(t) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} t - \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} \widehat{\sigma}(t) = \frac{1}{n!} \sum\limits_{\sigma} (t - \widehat{\sigma}(t)) \in J_{n}$
`\end{proof}`

> [!corollary|\*] Isomorphism of Symmetric Algebras
> The set of symmetric tensors is isomorphic to the $n$-th symmetric power of $M$
> $S_{n}(M) \cong S^{n}(M)$

`\begin{proof}`
This follows from the previous statement using noethers first isomorphism theorem since
$S^{n}(M) = \faktor{M^{\otimes n}}{J_{n}} = \faktor{M^{\otimes n}}{\ker (\mathrm{sym})} \cong S_{n}(M) $
`\end{proof}`

The previous corollary gives us that we can think of $S(M)$ as a submodule of $T(M)$. Specifically we can decompose each graded element into
$M^{\otimes n} = S_{n}(M) \oplus J_{n}$

> [!proposition|\*]
> If $M$ is free $R$-module with $\dim_{R}(M) = m \in \N$, then
> $S(M) \cong R[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{m}]$
> are isomorphic as $R$-algebras.
> We also have that $\dim_{R}(S^{k}(M)) = \binom{k+m - 1}{m-1} = \binom{k + m - 1}{k}$
> Additionally suppose $v_{1}, \ldots, v_{m}$ is a basis of $M$ then the image of $\set{v_{i_{1}} \otimes \ldots \otimes v_{i_{k}}}_{1 \leq i_{1} \leq \ldots \leq i_{k} \leq m}$ under $\mathrm{sym}$ is a basis of $S_{k}(M)$

`\begin{proof}`
The general proof idea is given is given by considering the basis of $M$ to be
$B = \set{v_{1}, \ldots, v_{m}}$
We then define an equivalence relation to $B^{k}$ by identitfying tuples that only differ by a permutation.
Then any symmetric multilinear map $\phi: M^{k} \to N$ will be constant on the equivalence classes. From here we can show that the free $R$-Module over $\faktor{B^{k}}{\sim}$ has the universal property of $S_{k}(M)$. From this we can prove that by uniqueness these must be isomorphic. On the other hand we can also show that the free $R$-Module over $\faktor{B^{k}}{\sim}$ is isomorphic to the set of degree $k$ monomials via an $R$-Module isomorphism.
`\end{proof}`
