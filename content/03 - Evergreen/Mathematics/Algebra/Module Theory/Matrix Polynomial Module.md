We consider for a given $\K$ vector space and a fixed linear map $0 \neq A \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\K}(V, V)$ and generate from this a $\K[x]$-[[Ring Modules|Module]].

> [!definition|\*] Matrix Polynomial Module
> Let $V \cong \K^{n}$ be a $\K$-vector space and $0 \neq A \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\K}(V, V)$ a vector space endomorphism. Then $V$ becomes a $\K[x]$-module via
> $p \cdot v = p(A) v$
> where $p(A)$ can be seen as evaluating the polynomial at matrices.

A central property here is connected to the [[Characteristic and Minimal Matrix Polynomials#Minimal Polynomia|minimal polynomial]] of $A$.

> [!proposition|\*] Matrix Polynomial Modules given by minimal Polynomials
> As a $K[x]$-module induced by a given endomorphism $0 \neq A \in \mathrm{Hom}_{\K}(V, V)$ the space $V$ does not have a basis. If the characteristic polynomial of $A$ is the minimal polynomial then the module is generated by one element

`\begin{proof}`
Suppose $\set{v_{i}}$ is a basis. Then for each $v \in V$ there should be a unique decomposition
$v = \sum\limits_{i} p_{i}(A) v_{i}$
From Cayley-Hamilton we know that that $\chi_{A}(A) = 0$. So we can construct
$\tilde{p_{i}} = p_{i} + \chi_{A}$
then since $\tilde{p_{i}} \neq p_{i}$ we get that
$\sum\limits_{i} \tilde{p_{i}} (A) v_{i} = \sum\limits_{i} \left( p_{i}(A) + \chi_{A}(A)\right) v_{i} = v$
meaning our decomposition is not unique.

Now assuming that $\deg( \mathrm{minpoly}(A)) = n$ there is a $x \in V$ such that
$\set{A^{k} x}_{k =0}^{n-1}$
is a linearly independent set. So for every $v \in V$ there are coefficients $c_{k} \in \K$ such that
$v =\sum\limits_{k = 0}^{n-1} c_{k} A^{k} x = p(A) x$
where we notice that this gives us the polynomial
$p(x) = \sum\limits_{k=0}^{n-1}c_{k} x^{k}$

`\end{proof}`
