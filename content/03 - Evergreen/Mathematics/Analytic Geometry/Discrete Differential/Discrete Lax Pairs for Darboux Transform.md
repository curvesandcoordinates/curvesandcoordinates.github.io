When considering specifically the [[Discrete Darboux Transform]] on curves in the [[Complex Projective Line]] we use the defining property, namely that the darboux transform $\tilde{\gamma}$ of $\gamma$ has to satisfy for a constant $\mu \in \C$
$\mathrm{cr}(\gamma, \gamma_{1}, \tilde{\gamma_{1}}, \tilde{\gamma}) = \mu$
Now this condition is non-linear and leads to difficult formulae for say the Projective curvature of the $\tilde{\gamma}$.

To simplify this calulcation and get into the area of integrable systems we want to consider a new approach.

## General Transport Matrices

Define a map
$F: \Z^{2} \to GL(n)$
and consider the strip given by the original curve and the darbour transform
$ \begin{CD} \tilde{\gamma}   @>>> \tilde{\gamma_{1}} @>>> \tilde{\gamma_{2}} \\ @AAA @AAA @AAA\\  \gamma @>>> \gamma_{1} @>>> \gamma_{2} \end{CD}$
this strip gets assigned a matrix for every vertex. This matrix acts like a local frame and we can begin to consider what properties we need of this family of matrice.

We assume that we can write the transition as follows

$$$\begin{align*}
F_{1} - F &= \tilde{U}F \\
F_{1} - F &= \tilde{V}F
\end{align*}$$
Now using $U = \tilde{U} +1$ and $V = \tilde{V} +1$ we get these two conceptual transport matrices
$$\begin{align*}
F_{1} = U F \\
F_{2} = V F
\end{align*}$$
Since we want the frame to be consistently defined along our strip we need to think of what condition $U, V$ have to have so that
$$VU F = F_{12} = F_{21} = UV F$$
meaning we want in general the matrices to commute:
$$UV = VU$$

Think of these matrices as living on the edge of each square.

## Application to Cross-Ratio Equations

Now lets get back to our general strip with the curve $\gamma$ and the darboux transform $\tilde{\gamma}$. We will write this as a 2D strip, and thus note that $z_{\cdot 2} = \tilde{\gamma_{\cdot}}$ and $z_{\cdot} = \gamma_{\cdot}$. Note that we can think of one quad as being
$$\begin{CD} z_{2}   @>e_{2}>> z_{12} \\ @A v AA @AA v_{1} A\\  z @>>e> z_{1}\end{CD}$$
Given that we know that each square must satisfy $\mathrm{cr}(z, z_{1}, z_{12}, z_{2}) = \mu$. Using this for our calculations we consider the Möbius transformation that is generated by fixing $z, z_{1}$ and $\mu$ and maps
$$v \to v_{1}$$
this is then given by
$$\mu = \frac{e e_{2}}{v v_{1}} = \frac{e(-v + e + v_{1})}{v v_{1}}$$
giving us an explicit map from $v$ to $v_{1}$ which gives us an explicit transport matrix for this edge
$$M_{e}(\mu) = \begin{pmatrix}1 & -e \\ -\frac{\mu}{e} & 1\end{pmatrix}$$
Doing this to the other edge transports gives us that the cross-ratio relation defining our darboux transport is actually equivalent to a matrix compatability equation in the above integrable systems way:


> [!theorem|*] Lax expression for Compatability Equations
> For the matrices
> $$\begin{align*}
> L_{n, m} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & -(z_{n+1, m} - z_{n,m}) \\ -\frac{\lambda \alpha_{n}}{z_{n+1, m}- z_{n,m}} & 1\end{pmatrix} \\
> M_{n, m} = \begin{pmatrix}1 & -(z_{n, m+1} - z_{n,m}) \\ -\frac{\lambda \beta{n}}{z_{n, m+1} - z_{n,m}} & 1\end{pmatrix}
> \end{align*}$$
> We have the compatability equation $M_{n+1, m} L_{m, n} = L_{m, n+1} M_{n,m}$ for all $\lambda$ and $n, m \in \Z$ if and only if $z$ satisfies
> $$\mathrm{cr}(z_{n, m}, z_{n+1, m}, z_{n+1, m+1}, z_{n, m+1}) = \frac{\alpha_{n}}{\beta_{m}}$$
> for all $n, m \in \Z$


Note in the above theorem that $\alpha$ only depends on $n$ and $\beta$ only on $m$, meaning the choice is easier.

### Lax Pairs in the Smooth Case
Given a PDE (say a matrix system)
$$F_{x} = UF, \quad F_{y} = V F$$
For some sequare matrices $U, V$. 
This PDE might not be solvable even. One condition that you can prove to ensure that the PDE is at least locally solvable 
$$F_{xy} = F_{yx}$$
But writing this out we get
$$(U_{y} + UV)F = U_{y} F + U F_{y} =  (F_{x})_{y} = F_{xy} = F_{yx} = (F_{y})_{x} = (VF)_{x} = V_{x} F + V F_{x} = (V_{x} + VU) F $$
So the condition actually states that
$$U_{y} - V_{x}.+ [U, V] = 0$$
Since this condition is something on $U, V$ it tells us that our PDE statements need to be compatables in its entries in some way, so that the solution even has local existence guaranteed. We call $(U, V)$ a Lax pair if such a compatability is given.
