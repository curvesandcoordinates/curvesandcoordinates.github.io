Hilbert’s Nullstellensatz is a central to the algebraic-geometry lexicon translating between the world of [[Affine Varieties|affine varieties]] and that of polynomial rings. Most simply stated we can summarise it in three parts

##

> [!theorem|\*] Correspondence Points - Maximal Ideals
> Let $\K$ be an algebraically closed field and $S \subset \K[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}]$ a set of polynomials. Let $\mathcal{M}_{S}$ be the set of all maximal ideals $\mathfrak{m} \subset \K[x_{1}, \ldots, x_{n}]$ with $S \subset \mathfrak{m}$. Then the map
> $\Phi: \mathcal{V}(S) \to \mathcal{M}_{S}, (\xi_{1}, \ldots, \xi_{n}) \mapsto (x_{1} - \xi_{1}, \ldots, x_{n} - \xi_{n})$
> is a bijection
